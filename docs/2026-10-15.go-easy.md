# Go easy on

The server's side of "things to go easy on" (heart-api#236; the app side is
heart-of-yours#396). In the app it is
an opt-in list of body areas and movements that suggests swaps. The research
is the Notion page "Programs — research", section 5.

## The user's list never leaves the phone

What someone says hurts or limits them is health-shaped, so it lives on the
device under the device-only health rule, the way health readings do:
- It never rides templates, workouts, upsync, exports or MCP.
- `Request.json()` refuses a body that carries it anywhere (`400
  health_data`). The keys `injur…`, `limitation…`, `goEasy…`, `painScore` and
  `painLevel` join the health-shaped list.
- `database/tests/public/health_rule.sql` refuses a column named for it. The
  two lists move together.

## What the server serves: the mapping

`content/go_easy.yml`, rendered by `scripts/go_easy_content.py` and uploaded by
hand as `static/go-easy`, like the other content objects. It holds:
- **Copy:** the screen's title, the explainer ("it stays on this phone and
  isn't medical advice") and the footer about normal discomfort.
- **Eight areas**, each with its name, its marker ("Easier on your knee"),
  `easier` and `prefer` predicates: lower back, knee, shoulder, wrist, neck,
  hip, ankle, elbow.
- **Three movements**, each with the same fields: jumping and impact, heavy
  weight on the spine, pressing overhead.

A predicate matches an exercise's library data: its pattern `groups`,
`axial_load`, `impact`, `stability`, and `category`. Every key must hold, any
listed value matching, and an entry matches when any of its predicates does.
The rendered file names the keys as heart_models' `Movement` and `Exercise`
do on the wire (`axialLoad`, not `axial_load`), so the app compares each one
with the field of the same name, and the values are those fields' own.
The checker refuses a value the library doesn't have, and a predicate that
matches no exercise. Tests pin the obvious cases: a deadlift is hard on the
lower back, a seated row isn't hard on the shoulder, a push-up is hard on the
wrist and a dumbbell press isn't.

**No new exercise tags.** The ticket expected new tags: loaded spinal flexion,
deep knee flexion, overhead and wrist load. The library's patterns already
say them:
- `trunk_flexion` covers loaded spinal flexion.
- `lunge_split` and `plyometric_lower` cover deep knee flexion.
- `vertical_press` covers overhead.
- Bodyweight presses and planks cover wrist load.

A tag would only be worth adding where a pattern can't tell two exercises
apart.

## How the app uses it

This section is a request, not a spec. Under the research's "modify and
monitor, not forbid":
- An area hides exercises from suggestions only.
- An exercise already in a plan gets a neutral marker and a one-tap swap,
  ranked by same pattern and kinder predicates.
- Nothing is blocked, no badge is red, and the word "injury" never appears.
- There's no free-text field, and no pregnancy rules yet.
- A program's own `constraint` key goes through the same check on the device.

## Gaps

- **Only library exercises match.** A predicate reads the library's movement
  data, so a custom exercise matches only by `category`, and is otherwise
  never flagged.
- **No balance group** in the library, so nothing can be "easier on balance"
  yet.
- **No pregnancy rules**, by choice, until there is research to write them
  from.
- **The mapping is unreviewed** by a clinician, and its `es`, `fr` and `ru`
  are machine-translated.
