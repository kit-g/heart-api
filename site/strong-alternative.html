Strong and Hevy are both good apps, and plenty of people are happy on either. If you're weighing a change, here's an honest look at how Heart compares: what it does, what it doesn't do yet, and how to bring your history along if you decide to try it.

Heart is a free workout log for iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Android. You can open it and train without an account.

Only what you turn on

Heart starts simple: a workout, its sets, and a timer. Most of the rest is a switch. RPE, the muscle map and the lock-screen workout are off until you want them, and the social features on the roadmap will work the same way. Two people can use Heart very differently, and both are using it right.

Your data works the same way. You don't need an account to log, everything can be exported at any time, and what Heart reads from your health app stays on your phone. The full list of what it does is on the features page.

Bringing your Strong history

Your past workouts can come with you, so your charts and records start where you left off.

  1. 1

    In Strong, open Profile → Settings → Export Strong Data. Strong emails you a CSV file. Save it somewhere your phone can reach.

  2. 2

    In Heart, sign in with Apple, Google or email. The import is saved to your account, so this is the one step that needs one. Anything you've already logged in Heart comes with you.

  3. 3

    Open Settings → Import workout history and pick the file. Heart shows how many workouts and sets are ready, and lists any exercises it doesn't recognise so you can choose which to bring over as your own.

Warm-up, drop and failure sets, RPE, exercise and workout notes, and each exercise's rest timer come across with the numbers. Importing the same file again is safe: workouts already in Heart are skipped, never duplicated, so you can import again later if you keep using both for a while.

Also worth a look

Heart won't be the right fit for everyone, and that's fine. A few others people often mention:

Hevy

If following friends and starting from a ready-made program matter most to you today, Hevy has both, along with Wear OS and a web app.

FitNotes

A plain, notebook-style log for Android, free and without ads, with backups to Google Drive or Dropbox.

Liftosaur

For lifters who run structured programs like 5/3/1 or GZCL: you write the progression rules in plain text, and the app applies them after each session. On iPhone, Android and the web.

Bazu

A newer iPhone and Apple Watch tracker built around next-session targets for progressive overload.

Questions

Is Heart really free?

Yes. Everything Heart does today is free, with no template or history limits and no ads.

Do I need an account?

Not to train. Heart works on your phone from the first launch. An account is for syncing between devices and for importing history, and signing in later brings everything you've logged with you.

What about my Strong templates?

Strong's export holds your workouts, not your templates, so those need setting up again. The quickest way is to open an imported workout and save it as a template.

Can I leave again?

Any time. Settings exports everything as CSV or JSON, offline, with or without an account, and you can erase it all from the same place.
